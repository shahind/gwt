Gym rules

  • RIR = repetitions in reserve. 10 reps @ 2 RIR means you finish believing you could have performed approximately two additional technically good repetitions.
  • Week 1: ~3 RIR. Week 2: ~2 RIR. Week 3 onward: ~1–2 RIR.
  • You don't need to deliberately reach failure on every set.
  • Once you reach the top of the rep range on every set with the prescribed RIR, increase the machine by the smallest practical increment.

Your most important exercises

A. Lateral raise

This is probably the single most visually important isolation exercise in your program. Your clavicle width cannot be changed, but increasing the lateral deltoid can make the shoulder appear substantially broader. Use a machine or cable if available. Don't turn it into a trap exercise by shrugging the weight upward.

B. Lat pulldown

This is probably your biggest training gap. You currently do essentially no lat training. Your photos show a reasonable structural shoulder-to-waist foundation but relatively little visible lat flare. Learn to actually pull with the elbow toward the hip, rather than simply pulling the handle toward your chest. You don't need enormous weight.

C. Chest-supported row

I particularly like this for you because: it builds upper back; trains scapular retractors; reduces the amount of stabilization your lower back has to perform; suits your preference for controlled equipment; and complements the lat pulldown. This is also preferable to immediately throwing conventional barbell rows into your program given your occasional back discomfort.

D. Incline machine press

Your upper chest is one of the clearest opportunities from the visual analysis. Use a moderate incline rather than turning it into an overhead press. Think approximately 20–35°.

Core and occasional back pain

I would not assume your back pain is simply because your core is weak. That is possible, but the photograph cannot establish the cause.

We're going to train the trunk while avoiding unnecessary spinal loading:

  • cable crunch
  • Pallof press
  • dead bug
  • controlled bracing
  • gradual loading
Important: If an exercise causes actual back pain rather than ordinary muscular effort/fatigue, stop that exercise and substitute it.

Your normal training already addresses much of what I saw:

More

Less reliance on

The goal isn't to permanently "pin your shoulders back." You want strong, controllable shoulders and trunk, not a forced posture.

Progression

This is where your new gym program needs to differ dramatically from your push-up training.

Double progression

Suppose your machine chest press is 3 × 6–10.

Start: 60 kg — 10, 9, 8

Next: 60 kg — 10, 10, 9

Then: 60 kg — 10, 10, 10

Once you can reach the top of the rep range on every set with the prescribed RIR, increase the machine by the smallest practical increment.

Then perhaps: 65 kg — 8, 8, 7, and build again.

Deloads

Don't automatically deload every fourth week.

Continue progressing until you see several of these simultaneously:

  • performance dropping
  • persistent fatigue
  • unusual joint soreness
  • motivation substantially falling
  • pumps/performance deteriorating
  • inability to recover between sessions

Then take approximately one easier week: reduce sets by ~30–50%, maintain moderate loads, and stay ~3–4 RIR.

For you, that might happen roughly every 6–10 weeks, but it should be driven by fatigue rather than the calendar.

The "1.5–2× current muscle volume" goal

If by this you mean your muscles looking 50–100% larger, that is not a realistic short-term target for someone who already has two years of training.

You can, however, make a very significant visual transformation because your recent training has been highly incomplete.

The biggest change may come from:

Push-ups → properly loaded chest + back + shoulders + legs + arms

rather than simply doing more push-ups.

The key priorities for your physique

  1. Train lats twice every week. You currently have a major pulling deficit.
  2. Train lateral delts twice every week. This is probably the most direct way to improve your shoulder-to-waist visual ratio.
  3. Bring upper chest up. Incline machine pressing gets dedicated volume.
  4. Start properly training legs. You've essentially left a large amount of potential muscle mass untrained for ~8 months.
  5. Keep your waist roughly where it is while gaining muscle. Don't chase either extreme—neither aggressive cutting nor aggressive bulking matches your stated aesthetic goal.

And importantly, you don't need to abandon machines to accomplish any of this. Your preference for controlled, muscle-specific exercises is compatible with the evidence-based approach; the critical variable is that the exercises are progressively overloaded and taken sufficiently close to muscular failure over time.

Why this plan fits you
